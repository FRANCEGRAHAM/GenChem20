01 · PROCEDUREExperimental Conditions & Preparation

  • All solutions were prepared with distilled water.
  • Zinc and copper electrodes were sanded before use.
  • Beakers were washed after every trial; solutions were stored appropriately.
  • Salt bridge: a Femme paper towel soaked in the salt-bridge solution.
The paper-towel bridge may have added resistance. This is a possible limitation, not a confirmed cause of the voltage drops. Other contributors: electrode polarization, contact resistance, wiring, electrolyte properties and the current drawn while charging.

Masses and volumes both changed between trials, so the effect of concentration cannot be isolated.
Meter reliability
The defective multimeter makes Test 1 readings uncertain; the instrument used later should be documented and checked against a known voltage.

06 · THEORYScientific Basis

Anode (oxidation): Zn(s) → Zn²⁺(aq) + 2e⁻
Cathode (reduction): Cu²⁺(aq) + 2e⁻ → Cu(s)
Overall: Zn(s) + Cu²⁺(aq) → Zn²⁺(aq) + Cu(s)
Electrons travel through the external path from zinc to copper; the salt bridge lets ions migrate to maintain electrical neutrality. The supercapacitors store charge at their electrode interfaces and do not perform the redox reaction.

07 · SUMMARYOverall Conclusion

The Zn–Cu cells generated measurable voltage, and the series connection produced more than either single cell. Capacitor 2’s initial failure was traced to incorrect breadboard wiring; once corrected, both capacitors showed voltage. Charging was slow and the source sagged under load, consistent with source and/or circuit resistance, but the data do not identify a single cause. The trials demonstrate the feasibility of the galvanic-cell and supercapacitor concept while documenting practical issues in wiring, measurement reliability and solution preparation.
